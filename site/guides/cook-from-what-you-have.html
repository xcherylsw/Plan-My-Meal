How to cook from what you already have

It's 5:30pm, nothing is planned, and the fridge is half full. You can almost always make a good meal from that.

1. Do a two-minute kitchen check

Open the fridge first, then the freezer, then the pantry. Note anything that won't last: leafy vegetables, minced meat, fish, tofu, open packs of anything. Those go first. Items that keep for weeks (rice, noodles, canned goods, onions, garlic) can wait.

2. Use a template, not a recipe

A meal is usually one protein, one or two vegetables, one carbohydrate and something savoury to tie them together. Pick one from each group:

ProteinVegetableCarbohydrateFlavour
Egg, tofu, chicken, fish, minced meatKailan, cabbage, spinach, long beans, tomatoesRice, noodles, pasta, breadGarlic and soy sauce, oyster sauce, curry paste, sambal, miso

Stir-fry, steam, simmer in soup, or bake. That covers most home cooking.

3. Keep five fallback dishes in your head

  • Egg fried rice, best with day-old rice, with whatever vegetables are left.
  • Stir-fried tofu and vegetables with garlic and a little oyster sauce.
  • Simple soup, such as egg and tomato, or tofu and miso, ready in 15 minutes.
  • Spaghetti aglio olio, which needs only pasta, garlic, oil and chilli.
  • Congee with a protein and spring onion, which also suits anyone who wants a soft meal.

4. Substitute without fear

Most vegetables swap for each other in a stir-fry (kailan, choy sum, cabbage, spinach); chicken, fish and tofu swap in many dishes with only a change in cooking time; any noodle works in a noodle soup. If one ingredient is missing, a close relative usually works. If the missing ingredient is what the dish is named after, choose a different dish instead.

5. Handle leftovers safely

Cool cooked food quickly and refrigerate it promptly rather than leaving it out for hours, which matters especially for rice and meat dishes. Reheat until piping hot all the way through, and reheat leftovers only once.

6. Keep a short staples list

A kitchen that always has rice, eggs, onions, garlic, soy sauce, oyster sauce, sesame oil, cooking oil, salt, sugar, pepper, noodles or pasta, and a few frozen vegetables can always produce dinner. Restock these before anything else.
