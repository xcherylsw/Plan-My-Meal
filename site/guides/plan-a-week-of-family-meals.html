How to plan a week of family meals without the stress

Most meal-planning stress isn't cooking; it's deciding. A fixed ten-minute routine once a week removes most of it.

1. Start with who's eating, not what to cook

Write down, for each day, who will actually be at the table for lunch and dinner. A household of five rarely eats together five nights a week. Knowing that Tuesday dinner is for two and Saturday lunch is for six changes how much you buy and how much you cook, and it's the single biggest cause of both waste and "we ran out". Today Cook What lets you set the number of people per meal for exactly this reason.

2. Choose anchor dishes first

Pick the three or four dishes you're most sure about: the family favourite, the thing that's on offer this week, the long braise you'll cook on the weekend. Place them where they suit the schedule: slow dishes on days with time, quick ones on busy nights. Fill the remaining slots after.

3. Rotate proteins and cuisines

Instead of choosing dishes one by one, rotate: fish, chicken, egg or tofu, beef, a vegetarian night. Then vary the cuisine so the week doesn't feel repetitive. A plan that looks varied is far more likely to be followed.

4. Cook once, eat twice

Make a double batch of anything that reheats well, such as curries, braises, soups and sauces. Cook rice for two meals but cool and refrigerate the extra promptly. Cooked rice left out for hours is a common food-safety problem. Prepare vegetables for two days at once when you're already chopping.

5. Build the shopping list from the plan

List what each dish needs, then subtract what you already have. Group the result by where it's found in the shop: vegetables, meat and fish, dry goods, sauces. You'll make one trip instead of three and you won't buy a third bottle of soy sauce. A good list is the difference between a plan and a wish.

6. Leave a buffer

Plan five or six dinners, not seven. Leave a slot for leftovers, a takeaway night, or the day a plan falls apart. Plans that allow for real life survive; plans that don't are abandoned by Wednesday.

7. Review quickly on the day you shop

Before you shop, glance at what's about to expire. Move dishes that use those ingredients earlier in the week. Anything you couldn't find at the shop can usually be swapped rather than abandoned: replace the vegetable, keep the dish.
